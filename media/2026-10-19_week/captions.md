**LinkedIn**
This week a car hopped into the air, and half the internet asked the same question: so why don't we have flying cars yet?

The honest answer fits in three words.

Heavy. A car sits on the road for free. A flying car pays, in energy, for every second it stays up.

Hungry. Jet fuel stores roughly 12,000 Wh per kilo. Today's lithium-ion cells store about 250 (Aviation Week). That gap is the whole story for battery aircraft.

Hard. A good car wants weight, crash structure and wheels. A good plane wants lightness and wings. As one aviation museum curator told AP, combine them and you often get a bad car and a bad plane.

None of this means it won't happen; it means the hard part is engineering, not demos. What engineering problem would you rather see solved first?
